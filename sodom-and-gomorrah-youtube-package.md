There was a valley that looked like Eden... and a city where mercy was a crime.

Told by the fire in the voice of an African griot, this cinematic retelling of Genesis 13–21 follows Abram, a herdsman who left everything at God's call, and his nephew Lot, who chose the rich green plain of the Jordan and slowly moved his tents toward the gates of Sodom.

Inside those golden walls, feeding the hungry was against the law, judges sold justice for silver, and the cry of the oppressed rose to heaven. Beneath the great trees of Mamre, three mysterious visitors come to Abraham, and an old man dares to plead with the Judge of all the earth: fifty... forty-five... forty... thirty... twenty... ten.

Then two angels walk into Sodom at nightfall. A mob surrounds Lot's door. A blinding light. A desperate escape at dawn. One command: DO NOT LOOK BACK.

Fire and brimstone fall from the sky. Lot's wife looks back... and becomes a pillar of salt.

But from the ashes, God keeps His promise: a son named Isaac is born to ninety-year-old Sarah, and a line that leads to Ruth, King David and a night in Bethlehem beneath a different star.
