# Analysis + Style DNA

## Niche
Explainers on economic geography and history. Every video asks one question: how did the land, location or resources (or the lack of them) decide a nation's wealth? The economics always stays tied to a physical place on the map, never to abstract theory.

## Target Audience
Curious adults, roughly 18–45, mostly English-speaking. They like history and geopolitics but have no economics training. They want surprising "why" answers with real numbers, delivered without jargon.

## Hook Style
- The assumption flip: open with what common sense predicts, then hit it with a contradicting fact.
- A present-day news anchor in the first sentence, which then pivots to the deeper past.
- Image stacking: three to five very short fragments, each one a single vivid picture.
- A "here's the strange part" pivot within the first 30 seconds that states the paradox and the thesis.
- Two or three teaser bullets that preview specific shocking facts from later in the video.

## Script Flow
1. Paradox hook: common belief, contradicting reality, thesis line.
2. Setup of the starting conditions: who had what, and when.
3. A chain of escalating segments. Each one follows: obstacle → hidden mechanism → turning point (a dated event or a named person) → hard number → a one-line reframe.
4. Subscribe ask at about 45–55%, phrased as a humble favor, followed by a bridge sentence back into the story.
5. Counter-case showing the opposite path.
6. Present-day callback to the opening image.
7. An honesty beat that adds a nuance or caveat.
8. Scorecard recap in fixed categories: Geography / Resources / History / Decisions.
9. A thesis restated as an inversion.
10. End-screen hook into the next video, with no sign-off.

## Sentence Rhythm
About 11 words per sentence on average, but the variance carries the rhythm: long data sentences of 20–30 words alternate with 2–5 word punches. Fragments are used freely. Colons do the reveals. Numbers are written as speech. A one-sentence paragraph closes nearly every segment.

## Tone
Calm, confident, quietly amused by irony. Authoritative but never preachy. Moral weight is stated flatly in one clause, with no editorializing. Hedges like "by one famous estimate" and "reportedly" signal rigor.

## Transitions
- Chain links: the last line of one segment points to the next.
- Rhetorical-question pivots.
- Directional cues: "Now look back north", "Now compare", "Meanwhile".
- Ranked countdown markers buried inside a hook sentence, never announced bare.
- Time jumps using exact years as the first words of a sentence.

## Curiosity Gaps
An entity is introduced before its name is revealed. An answer is delayed by a sentence or two. Promised payoffs are planted early. Myths are corrected. "Here's the part almost nobody mentions" opens a mini-gap mid-segment. An open loop at the end sends viewers to the next video.

## Emotional Triggers
Irony and reversal; underdog satisfaction; injustice, stated quietly; scale awe; present-tense threat; gamble and luck.

## Retention Techniques
A countdown or segmented structure that implies a payoff. A hard number in nearly every segment, with comparisons the viewer can picture. Micro-cliffhangers. Pattern interrupts at segment close. A callback to the opening image. The scorecard gives a sense of completion before the next-video hook.

## Direct Address
Light and strategic: "you" appears at hooks and pivots. Never "guys". The only "we" is in the subscribe ask.
