The fire did not rise from the earth. It fell from the sky.

At sunrise, over a valley that once looked like Eden, heaven opened... and burning sulfur poured down like rain on two great cities. Sodom. And Gomorrah.

On the road below, a mother was running for her life. Behind her, her whole world was screaming. An angel had given her one command. Do not look back.

She looked back.

And she never moved again.

How does a city of gold become a city heaven can no longer bear? Why would God bargain with one old man... for the lives of only ten? And what kind of law turns mercy into a crime?

Come close to the fire, my children. This is the story of Sodom and Gomorrah.

...

After the great flood, God set his bow in the clouds. Never again would the waters destroy the world. That was the promise.

But heaven made no promise about fire.

Then the voice of God came to a herdsman seventy-five years old. His name was Abram.

"Leave your land and your father's house," the Lord said, "and go to the land I will show you. Through you, every family of the earth will be blessed."

Abram knelt on the red earth. "Here I am, Lord."

He took his wife Sarai, his cattle, and young Lot, the son of his dead brother Haran, whom he raised as his own.

...

In Canaan, Abram's herds covered the hills like low clouds, and Lot grew rich beside him. But the land grew too small for so much blessing, and their herdsmen fought over the wells.

So Abram said, "Let there be no quarrel between us, for we are brothers. If you go left, I will go right. If you go right, I will go left."

The first choice belonged to the elder. But Abram gave it away.

Lot lifted his eyes to the plain of the Jordan. Green like the garden of Eden. Rich like Egypt along the Nile.

"I choose the whole plain," Lot said.

His wife smiled. "Cities with walls. I am tired of shaking sand out of my hair."

At the parting, Abram said, "My road goes up, Lot. Yours goes down. Wherever you go... raise your altar."

Season after season, Lot moved his tents closer... until he pitched them near Sodom.

Now the men of Sodom were wicked. Great sinners before the Lord.

...

But one market morning, an old stranger begged for bread, and the crowd laughed.

"In Sodom, each man eats what is his own," a neighbour told Lot. "It is the law."

"What law punishes mercy?"

"The law that made us rich."

Days later, Lot watched a man whipped in the square. His crime? He had fed the hungry... for the second time. The third time meant fire.

Lot stayed. And Lot kept silent.

He rose to sit among the elders at the city gate. One morning a poor farmer knelt before them. "I owed him thirty pieces of silver, and he took my field, worth three hundred!"

Lot saw a leather pouch slide into the chief judge's hand.

"The case is closed."

"But the debt was only thirty..." Lot began.

"Say it again, Hebrew... and the next case judged here will be yours."

Lot did not say it again.

At his table, the young men promised to his daughters mocked him. "If your God wanted to punish Sodom, old man, he would have come long ago!"

Even Lot's wife laughed. Lot did not.

Every cry, every whip, every door shut on a stranger rose like smoke... until the outcry of Sodom reached heaven.

...

In the heat of the day, beneath the great trees of Mamre, Abraham lifted his eyes. Three men stood before him. No dust had risen behind them.

Abraham ran and bowed to the ground. "My lords, rest beneath this tree. Sarah! Quickly! Three measures of fine flour!" He served them his finest calf.

"Where is Sarah, your wife?" the visitor asked. "When this season comes again, Sarah will have a son."

Behind the tent wall, old Sarah laughed to herself.

"Why did Sarah laugh? Is anything too hard for the Lord?"

"I did not laugh," Sarah said, trembling.

"No. You did laugh."

Then two of the men turned toward Sodom. The third remained. And Abraham knew... it was the Lord.

"The outcry against Sodom and Gomorrah is great," the Lord said, "and their sin is very heavy. I will go down and see."

...

Somewhere in that valley... was Lot.

Abraham stepped forward, trembling. "Will you destroy the righteous with the wicked? What if there are fifty righteous in the city? Shall not the Judge of all the earth do what is right?"

"For fifty, I will spare the whole city."

"I am only dust and ashes... but what if there are forty-five?"

"I will not destroy it."

"Forty? ... Thirty? ... Twenty?"

"For their sake, I will not destroy it."

"Let not the Lord be angry. Just once more. What if only ten are found?"

"Even for ten... I will spare it."

When Abraham opened his eyes, the hill was empty.

"Let them be there, Lord," he whispered. "Let them be there."

...

At dusk, two angels reached Sodom. Lot rose at the gate and bowed. "My lords, come to my house. You do not know this city at night."

He baked them unleavened bread. But before they lay down, the whole city came. Every man of Sodom, young and old, with torches, surrounding the house.

"Where are the men who came to you tonight? Bring them out to us... that we may know them!"

Lot went out alone and shut the door. And in his desperation, he spoke the language Sodom had taught him.

"My brothers, do not do this wicked thing! I have two daughters... only leave these men alone, for they came under the shelter of my roof!"

"This foreigner wants to be our judge! We will do worse to you than to them!"

The crowd surged forward. But the angels pulled Lot inside... and a blinding light struck every man at the door.

"Who else do you have here?" the angels asked. "Get them out. The Lord has sent us to destroy this place."

Lot warned the young men promised to his daughters. They only laughed. "Go back to sleep, old man."

...

At dawn, Lot lingered among his silver and his treasures. So the angels seized him, his wife, and his two daughters by the hand, because the Lord was merciful to him, and led them out of the city.

"Run for your lives! Do not look back! Do not stop anywhere in the plain! Flee to the mountains!"

"I cannot reach the mountains," Lot begged. "Let me flee to that little town. Is it not small?"

"Very well. Hurry! I can do nothing until you arrive there."

They ran. Lot's wife ran last.

"Lot! The gold... our daughters' dowry... everything we are is back there!"

"Everything we are is running, woman!"

The sun had risen when Lot entered Zoar.

Then the Lord rained burning sulfur and fire from heaven on Sodom and Gomorrah... on the whole plain, and everyone who lived there.

"Father! Where is Mother?"

Behind them, on the road... Lot's wife had looked back.

And she became a pillar of salt.

"Do not look, my daughter," Lot wept. "Do not look."

...

Early the next morning, Abraham climbed his hill. Smoke rose from the land like smoke from a furnace.

There had not been ten.

But God remembered Abraham... and brought Lot out of the destruction.

...

Afraid to stay in Zoar, Lot went up into a mountain cave with his two daughters.

"Our father is old," said the elder, "and there is no man left in the land. Let us give him wine, so his family line will not end."

What happened in that cave, the darkness kept.

The elder daughter bore Moab, father of the Moabites. The younger bore Ben-Ammi, father of the Ammonites.

...

And at Mamre, at the very time God had promised, ninety-year-old Sarah gave birth to a son.

"God has made me laugh," she said, "and everyone who hears will laugh with me!"

They named him Isaac... he laughs.

Abraham carried the child up the hill above the grey valley. "Down there, my son, I learned that the Judge of all the earth does what is right. And here in my arms... I am learning that he keeps what he promises."

...

Generations later, Ruth the Moabite gleaned grain in Bethlehem, and became great-grandmother of David, the shepherd boy who faced a giant. And an Ammonite princess bore a king to David's son, Solomon.

The line of the wound and the line of the promise ran side by side... until one holy night in Bethlehem, beneath a different star.

The fire consumed everything in that valley. But one thing still stands, forever.

The word of the Lord. What he promised... he fulfilled.
